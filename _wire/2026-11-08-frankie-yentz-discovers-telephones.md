*Years of selective silence end the moment a trade with Glen Hutchins threatens to collapse.*

For five years, the AAA has operated under the assumption that Frankie Yentz suffers from a rare medical condition preventing him from participating in GroupMe.

Messages go unanswered. Conversations come and go. Entire controversies develop, reach their conclusions, and become league history without Frankie contributing a single coherent thought.

The man somehow manages to field a competitive fantasy football team while maintaining roughly the same level of communication as a North Korean submarine.

Then somebody threatened his trade with Glen.

**And suddenly, Frankie became the most socially available man in America.**

### A miracle in Ohio

In what medical professionals will surely describe as an unprecedented recovery, Frankie discovered that his phone was capable of doing considerably more than displaying fantasy scores.

He could send messages.

He could make calls.

He could apparently contact people individually, engage in lengthy conversations, and pursue the commissioner with the determination of a collections agency.

Larry took Frankie's call while he was at work.

Glen took Frankie's call while he was looking for work.

Casey was apparently expected to return Frankie's call immediately after finishing work, and Frankie was becoming increasingly concerned that she had failed to respect his schedule.

**"You said your meetings were done at 5."**

This was Frankie Yentz.

The same Frankie Yentz who recently assured the league that he reads every GroupMe message, despite almost never demonstrating any evidence of doing so.

The same Frankie whose participation has become so infrequent that league members have openly discussed replacing him.

Now he's personally contacting the commissioner about trade policy.

Turns out Frankie never had a communication problem.

**He had a motivation problem.**

### Glen Hutchins: A man in desperate need of more talent

The controversy itself is hardly surprising.

Glen already possesses a roster that looks like the result of someone accidentally enabling unlimited trades in franchise mode.

Having previously acquired Brock Bowers in a transaction that continues to raise questions about Macon's judgment, Glen apparently decided the appropriate response to his competitive advantage was to acquire even more talent.

Chance objected, explaining that the transaction would make Glen's already stacked team even better.

Glen, as always, presented himself as an innocent participant in a free and fair marketplace.

He insisted that he had offered fair trades to everyone.

**"Except maybe my brother."**

A remarkable admission from the league's leading advocate for ethical trading.

Glen then suggested changing the veto rules, insisted that the league was being unfair, and eventually offered $20 to have the trade pushed through administratively.

Nothing says confidence in the fairness of a transaction quite like offering a cash payment to bypass the voting process.

But the real revelation was Frankie's behavior.

### Democracy is important when Frankie needs something

For years, the rest of the league has struggled to get Frankie to participate in even the simplest conversations.

Now, with a transaction hanging in the balance, he had apparently transformed himself into a professional political lobbyist.

Frankie wasn't just defending a trade.

He was contacting voters, conducting outreach, chasing down the commissioner, and demanding attention from people who had previously been lucky to get a reaction out of him.

This is more effort than Frankie has devoted to league communication in several seasons combined.

And naturally, all of it was motivated by something that directly benefited Frankie.

If he'd demonstrated this level of enthusiasm for ordinary league business, there might never have been discussions about replacing him.

Instead, the league has discovered that **the key to getting Frankie involved is to threaten something he wants.**

Glen, meanwhile, continued his campaign for the trade by posting inspirational quotations supposedly from George Washington and Abraham Lincoln.

According to Glen, Lincoln once proclaimed:

**"No one trades more fairly than Glen Hutchins."**

An impressive endorsement from a president who died more than a century before fantasy football existed.

Unfortunately, it may be the most credible piece of evidence Glen has offered in support of his trading practices.

### An inspirational story with an inevitable ending

The newsroom commends Frankie for his sudden commitment to league participation.

It is encouraging to see a longtime member rediscover the importance of communication, democratic engagement, and maintaining relationships with fellow managers.

Of course, all available evidence suggests that the moment his trade situation is resolved, Frankie will disappear back into the Ohio wilderness, never to be heard from again.

His phone will return to Do Not Disturb.

GroupMe will return to being something he supposedly reads.

And league members will once again have to wonder whether Frankie is still alive, still playing fantasy football, or simply waiting for another transaction to require his attention.

**AAA INSIDER FINDING:** After five years of silence, Frankie Yentz has conclusively demonstrated that he knows how to use a phone. Unfortunately, the feature only appears to activate when there is something in it for him.

*The newsroom recommends threatening another trade if anyone needs to get ahold of Frankie before Christmas.*
