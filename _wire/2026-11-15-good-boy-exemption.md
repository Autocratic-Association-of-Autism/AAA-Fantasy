The AAA has long maintained that every manager competes under the same rules.

That principle suffered a minor administrative adjustment Monday night.

When Ryan Hutchins encountered another lineup problem, Ryan Mays generously allowed him to correct the mistake.

Hutchins then asked how many similar corrections everyone else was permitted.

The answer was straightforward.

**"0"**

Naturally, Hutchins wanted to know why he qualified for an exception.

The commissioner of this particular arrangement provided a detailed explanation.

**"Youve been a very good boy."**

And with those words, a new era of fantasy football governance began.

### Merit-based favoritism

For years, the league has debated competitive integrity, roster management, veto procedures, and the proper application of rules.

Apparently, none of that was necessary.

All we needed was a system that determines whether a manager has been a good boy.

Under the emerging policy, managers may receive special privileges based on behavior, general agreeableness, and the willingness of Ryan Mays to grant them.

The newsroom sees considerable potential.

Future disputes could be resolved through simple questions.

Did you behave yourself?

Were you polite?

Have you been a good boy?

If the answer is yes, perhaps the rules need not apply.

If the answer is no, you should have considered being better behaved.

### Concerns about fairness

Some members may argue that allowing one manager a lineup correction while granting everyone else zero exceptions constitutes preferential treatment.

This criticism fails to account for one important factor.

**Ryan Hutchins was a very good boy.**

The distinction was clearly communicated, the decision was made, and no further explanation should be necessary.

AAA Insider has also determined that Ryan Mays acted with remarkable confidence and decisiveness, which are important qualities in any administrator.

Whether this system will be extended to other managers remains unclear.

Chance Robinson, who recently announced himself as commissioner, may wish to examine the policy more closely.

Unfortunately, threatening Zak's kneecaps suggests that Chance would currently struggle to qualify.
