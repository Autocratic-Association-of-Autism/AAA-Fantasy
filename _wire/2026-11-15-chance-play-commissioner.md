After months of campaigning, numerous failed promises, and a growing inability to distinguish fantasy from reality, Chance Robinson has finally accomplished something.

**He has convinced himself that he is the commissioner.**

The breakthrough came after Chance created a series of unofficial GroupMe polls, interpreted their results however he pleased, and announced that the league had reached a final decision.

Unfortunately, the actual commissioner disagreed.

Casey reminded Chance that he possessed absolutely none of the authority he was attempting to exercise.

Chance responded by explaining that the people had spoken.

When the polling concluded, he announced:

**"I will now claim my rightful place as commissioner."**

The newsroom believes it is important that the league handle this situation with patience, compassion, and encouragement.

Chance has clearly put considerable effort into his little election.

He made the polls himself. He asked everyone to participate. He even counted the votes.

**And shouldn't that be enough?**

### Celebrating the little victories

Experts in managing fragile egos generally recommend acknowledging even modest accomplishments.

For Chance, creating three GroupMe polls represents a meaningful step toward understanding how organized government works.

Rather than discourage him by explaining that unofficial polls do not transfer constitutional authority, the league should consider allowing him to continue this confidence-building activity.

He can announce himself as commissioner.

He can give speeches.

He can even have a special title if it makes him feel important.

We just shouldn't let him touch anything.

In fact, Chance's first official executive action suggests he may already understand his limitations.

He immediately delegated **all actual league responsibilities to Casey Hutchins and Ryan Mays**, while reserving the position of "face of the league" for himself.

An excellent arrangement.

The adults continue handling league operations, while Chance receives the recognition and encouragement he so desperately needs.

### The importance of positive reinforcement

Going forward, AAA Insider recommends that league members respond supportively whenever Chance announces an imaginary executive decision.

A simple "Great job, buddy!" or "That's a very official-looking poll!" could go a long way toward maintaining his confidence.

There is no need to point out that Casey remains commissioner.

There is no need to explain that some of his strongest supporters aren't even members of the league.

And there is certainly no reason to confront him with the fact that his commissioner election included a poll asking whom people would **least** want in charge.

These are complicated concepts.

We can introduce them gradually.

There is, however, one concern.

On Sunday morning, Chance suggested that Zak Wesolek's kneecaps might be in danger if he defeated the self-appointed commissioner.

This is an unfortunate escalation for someone whose administration currently exists entirely in his imagination.

The league should probably explain that threatening political opponents is not an appropriate use of his new pretend authority.

But otherwise, he's doing wonderfully.

**AAA INSIDER FINDING:** Chance has worked very hard on his commissioner project, and everyone should be proud of his progress. The newsroom recommends a participation certificate, a ceremonial desk nameplate, and continued supervision by Casey.
