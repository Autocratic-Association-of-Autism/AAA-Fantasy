*Five years without a title have apparently driven the commissioner hopeful to the supernatural.*

There comes a point in every fantasy manager's career when conventional strategy has failed so spectacularly that alternative methods must be considered.

Waiver claims. Trades. Lineup adjustments. Actually drafting good players.

Chance Robinson has apparently exhausted these options and moved directly to **communicating with the dead.**

The five-year championship drought has finally taken its toll on the man who wants to be the next commissioner of the AAA.

Rather than simply hoping his opponents have bad games like a normal person, Chance has begun openly threatening to summon spirits and cast spells on opposing players.

When Glen complimented Frankie's lineup Saturday morning, Chance's response was immediate:

**"I'm going to conjure the spirits and fix that."**

By Sunday morning, the situation had escalated considerably.

"The list has been made," Chance announced, apparently referring to a collection of NFL players he intended to place under supernatural surveillance.

Frankie immediately asked whether his entire roster had been targeted.

Chance assured him that it had not, while specifically identifying George Pickens and Brandon Aubrey as players he had graciously spared.

Which raises an important question.

**What the fuck is Chance Robinson doing with a handwritten list of professional athletes he intends to curse?**

### A troubling development in the commissioner race

This is particularly concerning behavior from a man who has spent much of the season campaigning on integrity.

Candidate Robinson has promised stronger leadership, a better league, and an entirely theoretical prize structure funded through the power of friendship.

Now, apparently, the campaign has expanded its platform to include spiritual warfare.

The newsroom has reviewed the AAA constitution and found no explicit prohibition against witchcraft.

This is likely because, when the rules were written, nobody anticipated that a grown man would attempt to win a fantasy football championship by putting a hex on Tee Higgins.

The bigger concern is how casually Chance has embraced this newfound power.

When Ryan Hutchins demanded that he put the entire league under enchantment, Chance admitted that he had nearly included everyone's roster.

**Everyone's roster.**

Not just his opponent. Not just a rival. Every single team.

This is a man asking the league to give him administrative authority while openly entertaining the idea of using paranormal forces against the entire membership.

### Unfortunately, the magic appears to be defective

The most embarrassing development came when Ja'Marr Chase scored a touchdown.

Chance, discovering that his supernatural operations had failed to anticipate an entirely predictable NFL event, admitted:

"I should have added one more name to the list."

An extraordinary statement from a man claiming access to the spirit world.

Apparently, Chance can communicate with forces beyond human comprehension, but still cannot identify every player who might score a touchdown.

Then again, poor preparation has been a recurring theme throughout his fantasy career.

Five years. Zero championships.

Perhaps the spirits are simply better at evaluating his roster than he is.

The newsroom cannot independently verify that the supernatural exists, that Chance has successfully cursed a single player, or that the dead have any interest in participating in a fantasy football league.

But one thing is certain.

**If Chance Robinson can summon the spirits of the departed, he should probably start by asking one of them how to win a championship.**
