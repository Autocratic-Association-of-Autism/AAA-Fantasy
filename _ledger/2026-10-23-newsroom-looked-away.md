*By the AAA Insider Newsroom*

In one week, a candidate for commissioner was publicly dismantled, the sitting commissioner discovered the concept of winning, three teams got dangerously close to losing the right to choose their own names, and Ryan conducted enough trades to furnish a second fantasy league.

AAA Insider has examined the available evidence and reached a preliminary conclusion: **there is no functioning adult supervision anywhere in this organization.**

The league declined to appoint an adult, citing concerns about competitive balance.

## CHANCE CAMPAIGNS ON CHANGE; RECEIVES 41.88 POINTS OF IT

Chance Robinson entered Week 6 undefeated at 5–0, a record that had become a fairly significant part of his argument for running the league.

Then **Lamborghini Murci** beat **Njigba please**, **119.08–77.20**.

That's a **41.88-point** loss. For context, in a league where defeats of more than 35 points carry team-name consequences, this was not simply a loss. It was an application for a new identity.

The beneficiary was Ryan Hutchins, whose team improved to 4–2 and who can now add *person who beat Chance* to an already ambitious collection of things he would like everyone to know about Ryan Hutchins.

The newsroom attempted to schedule a congratulatory interview with Hutchins, but feared it might become an ongoing podcast.

Chance remains 5–1 and very much in the standings race. His campaign's strongest talking point, however, has been downgraded from *undefeated* to *please focus on the other five games*.

## CASEY HUTCHINS FINDS VICTORY UNDER A PILE OF ADMINISTRATIVE PAPERWORK

At the opposite end of the standings, sitting commissioner Casey Hutchins had spent five weeks demonstrating that governing a league and participating successfully in it are separate qualifications.

Week 6 changed that.

**ChanceforChange2027** defeated Ryan's **Big Ditka Energy**, **132.58–97.38**. The win was Casey's first of the season. The margin was **35.20 points**—just enough to cross the league's embarrassing-loss threshold, with twenty hundredths of a point to spare.

Political analysts are calling it a mandate. The standings are calling it 1–5.

Ryan, now 2–4, served as the unfortunate supporting cast in an important moment for the institution. Some may call it defeat. Others might call it an unusually generous contribution to stable league government. AAA Insider's editorial board favors a nuanced interpretation.

It is worth noting that Casey managed to beat a team by more than 35 points during the same week her principal challenger lost by more than 40. Whoever is writing the campaign advertisements has been handed material that should not require additional invention.

## THE WEEK'S OTHER VICTIMS REQUEST LESS PRESS COVERAGE

**Champ** (Glen Hutchins) defeated **la porta potty** (Louis Swanson), **108.86–69.72**. Glen is 5–1. Louis is 1–5 and posted **69.72**, a number with comedic properties that his opponents will undoubtedly handle with maturity.

The margin was **39.14**, placing Louis alongside Chance and Ryan in Week 6's exclusive *You May No Longer Own Your Team Name* discussion. Whether any names actually changed remains a separate matter for the newsroom to verify.

**Warren Hormuz** (Zak Wesolek) scored **146.60** against **Kenneth Skywalker** (Larry Terrell), who scored **135.74** and lost anyway. Zak had Week 6's highest score; Larry put up enough points to beat most of the league and was rewarded with a defeat. If Larry is looking for a legitimate grievance, this one comes with ESPN documentation.

Meanwhile, **Coke(r) Caine** (Macon Moore) defeated **Coach** (Frankie Yentz), **117.04–100.90**. Macon reached 4–2. Frankie fell to 2–4. Against all expectations, Macon successfully conducted a football transaction that did not require another manager's approval: scoring points.

## RYAN REPLACES HALF HIS LOCKER ROOM WHILE EVERYONE ELSE IS DISTRACTED

On October 20, Ryan shipped **Bijan Robinson and Brian Robinson Jr.** to Chance for **Jaxon Smith-Njigba, J.K. Dobbins, and Emanuel Wilson**.

On October 21, he sent **Caleb Williams and Hunter Henry** to Ryan Hutchins for **Bhayshul Tuten and Rachaad White**. He then sent **Alvin Kamara and Jameson Williams** to Macon for **Chuba Hubbard**.

Three completed trades. Three different negotiating partners. Six players out, six players in.

At this stage, the only thing definitely remaining from Ryan's original roster might be the team owner. Even Emanuel Wilson, acquired in the first deal, was dropped the following day. His tenure was shorter than an average Macon trade conversation.

The effectiveness of this remodeling project has not yet been determined. But unlike Chance's campaign, Ryan has already delivered a measurable quantity of change.

## CHANCE'S FINANCIAL PLAN IS SENT TO THE FRIENDSHIP DEPARTMENT

During an October 17 GroupMe exchange about offering bigger prizes without increasing league dues, Chance supplied a phrase that has since attracted the attention of the AAA Insider Budget Office:

**“The prize of friendship.”**

He later promised **“great prizes.”** The newsroom has been unable to locate a spreadsheet explaining how friendship becomes cash, although Ryan Hutchins is believed to possess an extremely large amount of self-appreciation, which may be a related asset.

Meanwhile, an anonymous email has attempted to convert informal talk of expanding AAA to 12 teams into a constitutional crisis. The tip alleges Larry and Frankie oppose expansion. **That claim has not been verified.** Chance supports expanding; Casey's position is unclear; and there is no formal proposal. At present, the most concrete thing about the controversy is the anonymous author's determination to have one.

## THE OFFICIAL SITUATION

Glen's **Champ** and Chance's team are 5–1. Macon's **Coke(r) Caine** and Ryan Hutchins's **Lamborghini Murci** are 4–2. Zak and Larry are 3–3. Frankie and Ryan are 2–4. Casey and Louis are 1–5.

The league's future is uncertain. Its ability to embarrass itself without warning is not.

**The case remains open. Nobody has been cleared of anything.**
