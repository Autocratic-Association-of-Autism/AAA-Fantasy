*By Ryan Mays — AAA Insider*

Larry Terrell is, by almost every conventional measure, a successful adult. He's married, has two young daughters, graduated from NC State, and works in property valuation for the State Employees' Credit Union. He also has 50 career fantasy football victories, a championship, and a winning percentage above .600.

It's an impressive résumé, particularly for a man who has made so many decisions that suggest he should not be left unsupervised.

To understand Larry Terrell, you have to go back before the fantasy championship, before the questionable team names, and before the unfortunate events of last Sunday. You have to go back to a younger Larry, who once posed for a photograph that AAA Insider believes may be the strongest evidence ever collected of an abandoned Christian rock career.

Look at that man. That is somebody who has absolutely asked a room full of teenagers to close their eyes while he strummed three chords on an acoustic guitar. You can practically hear him explaining that the next song is about a difficult season in his relationship with God. Whether Larry actually pursued a career in Christian music remains unconfirmed. What we do know is that somebody took that photograph, Larry approved of it, and the resulting image has survived long enough to become a problem for him.

It is especially difficult to reconcile that young man with the adult who would eventually name his fantasy football team **King of the Crips**. Somewhere along the way, Larry took a very unusual spiritual journey.

## The Making of Larry Terrell

Larry attended NC State, where he studied soil and water. For those unfamiliar with the field, this means Larry voluntarily spent several years learning about dirt at a level of detail the rest of us would consider deeply unnecessary. He later built a career in financial services and property valuation, which is perhaps the most surprising discovery of our investigation. Somewhere in North Carolina, people trust Larry Terrell to assess the value of their property while remaining completely unaware that he once referred to himself as Giga Chad Barney.

Despite everything, Larry appears to have established a respectable personal life. He married, started a family, and now has two young daughters. There is every reason to believe he's a responsible husband and father, although we have deliberately avoided interviewing anyone who might confirm this.

His wedding, however, deserves some attention. Weddings are generally remembered for the exchange of vows, the first dance, and two people beginning their lives together. Larry's wedding produced a slightly different collection of memories.

And Chance apparently wasn't the only guest interested in getting his hands on the groom.

## From Dippin' SKOL to The Prophet

Larry joined the league in 2021 under the name **Dippin' SKOL**, immediately identifying himself as a Minnesota Vikings fan. This explains quite a bit. The Vikings have appeared in four Super Bowls and lost every single one. For decades, Minnesota has specialized in convincing its supporters that something wonderful might finally happen before delivering some elaborate new form of disappointment. Larry, who lives in North Carolina and presumably could have chosen almost any football team to support, selected this experience voluntarily.

His first fantasy season suggested he had absorbed the franchise's philosophy perfectly. Larry finished **5–9**, seventh overall. In an attempt to salvage the season, he made an extraordinary **52 acquisitions**, an impressive amount of work to accomplish almost nothing. He was changing his roster constantly, searching for answers, and somehow making the same five-win season increasingly complicated.

But unlike his beloved Vikings, Larry eventually figured out how to win something. In 2022, he returned as **Giga Chad Barney** and improved to 7–7, sixth place. The name suggested enormous confidence for somebody who had achieved exactly as many wins as losses. Nevertheless, the trajectory was improving, and something genuinely remarkable was about to happen.

In 2023, Larry renamed his team **The Prophet**, went 10–4, secured the top playoff seed, and won the league championship. There is no getting around it: Larry was legitimately excellent. Three years after entering the league as a five-win disaster, he had become its best manager.

The manner in which he accomplished this, however, was occasionally ridiculous. During that championship season, Larry defeated Ryan Hutchins **98.30–98.24**. Six hundredths of a point. That's barely enough to survive a statistical correction, much less justify calling yourself a prophet. He also escaped Louis Swanson by just **0.66** points, suggesting his championship campaign involved considerably more begging than the final standings reveal.

Nevertheless, Larry won. The title was legitimate, and his progression from league embarrassment to champion remains one of the better turnaround stories in our history.

Unfortunately, winning a championship appeared to have a profound effect on his personality.

## A Champion Becomes a Problem

By 2024, Larry had established himself as one of the most consistently competitive managers in the league. Playing as **Hurts Hurt Me**, he finished 9–5 and earned the second playoff seed. In Week 14, he scored **182.82** points and defeated Macon Moore by **80.96**. This was not the same Larry who had made 52 roster acquisitions trying to win his sixth game. He was now capable of absolutely destroying somebody, and you can imagine how insufferable that made him.

His season eventually ended in a playoff matchup against Ryan Mays. Larry scored **160.14**, a performance that would ordinarily leave a manager feeling pretty good about his chances of advancing.

Ryan scored **182.92**.

There is something beautiful about a man playing one of his best games, looking at the scoreboard, and discovering that none of it mattered. Larry wasn't even particularly bad. Ryan was simply considerably better, an important distinction that AAA Insider believes deserves recognition. Larry finished third.

In 2025, he returned with perhaps the most inexplicable team name of his career: **King of the Crips**. It's worth remembering who we're discussing. Larry is a married father of two who works in property valuation, attended NC State, and once posed for what appears to be a Christian contemporary music promotional shoot. This man looked at the available branding opportunities and decided that his fantasy football team should be associated with a notorious American street gang.

We don't know what occasion inspired the white suit, but there is a confidence here that borders on a medical concern.

Despite his increasingly strange personal branding, Larry went **11–3**, his best regular-season record, and earned another second seed. Then he finished fourth. It was an unfortunate ending to a dominant regular season, but his larger body of work was becoming difficult to dispute. Three consecutive winning seasons, a championship, and a reputation as somebody nobody particularly wanted to face: for all his faults, Larry Terrell could play fantasy football.

<div class="larry-clear"></div>

Which makes the events of the past week even more remarkable.

## The Fall of Kenneth Skywalker

Larry entered 2026 under the relatively harmless name **Kenneth Skywalker**. Compared with The Prophet and King of the Crips, it was an unusually restrained choice, suggesting perhaps that marriage, fatherhood, and age were finally having a calming influence. He opened the season 2–1 and appeared headed toward another competitive year. In Week 4, he faced Ryan Hutchins, a longtime league acquaintance who also happened to attend Larry's wedding.

At the time of this photograph, Larry could not have known that the man beside him would someday be responsible for the most humiliating rebranding of his fantasy career. He probably should have been more concerned with whatever was happening between himself and Chance at the reception.

Before their Week 4 matchup, Larry had been enthusiastically discussing the possibility of defeating Hutchins badly enough to invoke the league's 35-point team-renaming punishment. By Sunday evening, that confidence had deteriorated into a desperate announcement: **“We will fight to not lose by 35.”**

They lost by nearly 40.

Hutchins defeated Larry **142.28–102.48**, a margin of **39.80**, earning the right to rename his opponent. Kenneth Skywalker was dead. In his place stood **Mangled Prison Pocket**. After years of calling himself The Prophet, Giga Chad Barney, and King of the Crips, Larry finally lost the privilege of choosing his own ridiculous identity. He had been renamed by somebody who attended his wedding.

And somehow, Hutchins wasn't finished talking about Larry.

<div class="larry-clear"></div>

Following the victory, he sent Ryan Mays an unsolicited account of his experience:

> “I found the matchup against Larry to be ‘pleasurable’. I hope you find your matchup to be the same.”

This is a deeply troubling thing to hear from the man who has just renamed somebody Mangled Prison Pocket. Hutchins didn't just beat Larry. He gave Mangled Prison Pocket a glowing review and then encouraged another man to have a turn. At this point, we're no longer sure whether Hutchins is offering fantasy football advice or attempting to arrange Larry's next romantic encounter. Either way, the enthusiasm is unsettling.

Ryan Mays appreciates the recommendation, although he'd prefer to keep the encounter strictly professional.

## The Man, the Myth, the Mangled Prison Pocket

It's worth remembering that Larry Terrell remains one of the league's most successful managers. Fifty career victories, a championship, and three consecutive excellent regular seasons are not accomplishments to dismiss. Outside fantasy football, he's built a career, a marriage, and a family. He is, by all available indications, an accomplished and reasonably functional member of society.

But over the course of researching his life, AAA Insider has learned that Larry studied dirt, voluntarily roots for the Minnesota Vikings, once looked like the opening act at a Christian youth conference, celebrated his wedding by being cradled in Chance Robinson's arms, dressed like an unsuccessful nightclub owner, and spent an entire season referring to himself as King of the Crips.

He also once won a fantasy game by six hundredths of a point against Ryan Hutchins, who has now returned the favor in rather emphatic fashion.

Larry Terrell, former champion and one of the most decorated managers in league history, now answers to **Mangled Prison Pocket**. He doesn't own Mangled Prison Pocket. **He is Mangled Prison Pocket.**

This week, he faces Ryan Mays. The matchup presents Larry with an opportunity to recover some dignity, although his previous playoff encounter with Ryan suggests dignity may be difficult to obtain.

As for why AAA Insider has chosen this particular week to take an extensive look into Larry's past, the timing is entirely coincidental.

We wish Larry the best.

Within reason.
