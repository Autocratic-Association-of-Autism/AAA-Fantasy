*By Ryan Mays — AAA Insider*

Week 4 was a showcase in avoidable humiliation, though not everyone participated equally. **Ryan Mays** beat Zak while leaving enough points on the bench to suggest he was actively managing for style points. Chance moved to 4–0, forcing the league to acknowledge that the man behind **Njigba Please** may actually be competent, which remains a deeply uncomfortable development. Casey stayed winless but at least refused to rig her own lineup, proving once again that the commissioner’s office has standards even when the roster does not. Hutchins beat Larry badly enough to rename his team **Mangled Prison Pocket**, while Glen beat Frankie by three after first losing a one-on-one matchup with the floor of his own house.

Five matchups. Multiple self-inflicted wounds. One undefeated team with a name that should probably be reviewed by counsel.

And Ryan Mays, against all odds and despite wasting half his bench, is right back where he belongs.

**Here is what happened.**

## BIJAN MUSTARD 99.30, WESO-HORNAY 89.44
### *Zak survives the profile. The matchup goes less well.*

AAA Insider spent Friday [introducing the league to Zak Wesolek]({{ '/ledger/2026-week4-meet-zak-wesolek.html' | relative_url }}). By Monday night, the newsroom had learned something new about him: **he can lose to Ryan Mays in under 100 points.** Bijan Mustard won **99.30–89.44**, moving Ryan to 2–2 and dropping Zak to the exact same record despite Zak entering the weekend with the better standing, the better 2025 résumé and roughly 1,500 freshly published words explaining why everyone should take him seriously. AAA Insider regrets any role it may have played in creating that impression.

Early Sunday, Ryan Hutchins asked whether AI had told Ryan to start Stefon Diggs over Denzel Boston. Boston had already scored **12.9** on Ryan’s bench while Diggs finished with **8.5**, so Hutchins did successfully locate four missing points. Congratulations to Ryan Hutchins on one of his strongest statistical discoveries since learning that he personally occupies half of the league’s ten-lowest-score leaderboard. Unfortunately, he stopped counting too early. Ryan also left **Brian Robinson Jr. (25.7), Alvin Kamara (22.8), Jameson Williams (16.2)** and **Colston Loveland (11.7)** on the bench. An optimized lineup could have approached **148 points**. Ryan scored 99.30 and still won. Some outlets might call that poor management. AAA Insider calls it **load management**. Ryan assessed the opponent, calculated the minimum necessary effort and refused to waste premium production on Zak Wesolek.

Zak, unfortunately, does not receive the same editorial protection. **Kyle Monangai scored 28.0 points on his bench**, more than enough to erase the final 9.86-point margin. Ryan also left **Colston Loveland’s 11.7** on the bench, so AAA Insider will not be adopting some fake “never bench a Bear” doctrine simply because it is convenient. The actual policy is simpler: **if you bench a Chicago Bear and still win, the matter is closed. If you bench a Chicago Bear who would have won you the matchup, you have made a grave error in judgment.** Ryan won. Zak did not. Monangai spent Sunday trying to rescue Weso-Hornay from the bench while Zak watched it happen in real time. That is not an indictment of benching Bears players. It is an indictment of **benching the wrong one when you needed 10 points.**

Rashee Rice also exited early and gave Zak a zero, which qualifies as legitimate bad luck. But “bad luck” is significantly less entertaining than “Zak left the winning points on his bench while losing to a team that could not crack 100,” so the newsroom will be emphasizing the latter.

### Monday Night Became a Formality

Ryan entered Monday ahead **71.60–53.90**, with Bijan Robinson facing Tyler Shough and Chris Olave. That technically constituted a live fantasy matchup. Then Bijan took Atlanta’s third offensive play **59 yards for a touchdown**. Casey immediately posted:

> “Bijannnnnn!!!”

Ryan replied:

> “Yessssssss”

This is the type of decisive executive support AAA Insider expects from the commissioner’s office.

Bijan scored again, finished with **27.7**, and Monday night became less of a comeback attempt and more of Zak watching Ryan enjoy television. The funniest part is that Tyler Shough was actually fine. AAA Insider had spent the [Weekend Preview]({{ '/ledger/2026-week4-weekend-preview.html' | relative_url }}) suggesting Shough would sink Zak. He did not. The newsroom has reviewed the failed prediction and determined that the analysis remains excellent. Zak simply developed alternative methods of losing.

Ryan and Zak’s previous meetings had been less “fantasy rivalry” and more “evidence locker”: Ryan won by **84.46 points in 2023**, Zak answered with a **78.58-point** beating in 2025, and Week 4 finally produced something resembling a normal game. Unfortunately for Zak, normal still meant losing.

Ryan improves to **2–2**. Zak falls to **2–2**.

Same record.

One of them also owns the website.

### What’s Next

Next week, Ryan gets Larry. Zak gets undefeated **Njigba Please**, which is now 4–0 and unfortunately also named like someone spent several minutes trying to determine how close they could get to a racial slur while still technically typing an NFL player’s name. AAA Insider does not approve of the branding, does not endorse the joke, and will be judging Chance accordingly every time the standings force us to type it.

AAA Insider wishes Zak luck.

Not enough to mean it.

## COKE(R) CAINE 128.06, CHANCEFORCHANGE2027 83.58
### *The Hospital Bowl ends with a blowout, an ethics investigation and absolutely no abuse of executive power*

Macon beat Casey **128.06–83.58** in what Macon himself accurately described Sunday afternoon as **“the hospital bowl.”** Between Casey’s inactive players, Macon’s injured receivers and seemingly half the NFL limping toward the blue medical tent, this matchup spent much of Sunday looking less like fantasy football and more like a workers’ compensation claim. Macon eventually emerged with a 44.48-point win, while Casey fell to 0–4 in a result AAA Insider has determined reflects absolutely nothing about her qualifications to continue governing the league.

Casey’s trouble started before kickoff. After a weekend that included her baby shower and the logistical burden of being extremely pregnant while still maintaining the basic functions of league government, she admitted Sunday morning that she had been packing the car, had not checked her lineup and accidentally left an inactive **Keenan Allen** in the starting lineup. Most leagues would see this as an unfortunate managerial mistake. Larry saw an opportunity to allege corruption. By early afternoon, he was accusing the commissioner of potentially using executive powers to remove Allen after kickoff. AAA Insider immediately opened an investigation. Casey responded: **“If I had done that I wouldn’t be nearly as miserable as I am rn.”** The newsroom reviewed the evidence and found Allen still sitting there with a beautiful **0.0**. The dictatorship case collapsed immediately. Casey may have lost by 44 points, but democracy survived.

Unfortunately, the rest of Casey’s roster then began participating in what increasingly looked like a coordinated work stoppage. **Saquon Barkley scored 2.0. Chase McLaughlin scored 2.0. Buffalo’s defense scored 1.0. Matthew Stafford gave her 9.68.** At one point Casey simply posted, **“RIP saquon,”** before asking the larger existential question: **“Am I cursed until this baby gets here?!?”** AAA Insider is not prepared to confirm supernatural interference, but with the baby due in November and Casey currently 0–4, the timeline is at least worth monitoring.

The cruelest part was that Casey actually had points. **Nico Collins scored 30.8 on her bench. Carnell Tate added 21.5.** An optimized version of her lineup would have scored **133.88**, enough to beat the lineup Macon actually started. AAA Insider therefore rejects the suggestion that Casey assembled a bad team. The points were present. They were simply stored in the wrong department, which is a completely normal thing for a functioning government to do.

Macon, meanwhile, received **36.7 from Kyren Williams, 25.9 from Chuba Hubbard and 21.46 from Bryce Young**, allowing him to survive a 0 from Ladd McConkey and only 2.7 from DJ Moore. Moore then injured his shoulder, prompting Macon to announce, “DJ Moore is made of glass,” which seems harsh but is considerably easier to say when Kyren Williams is busy dropping 36 on a pregnant woman.

Chance, observing the wreckage, offered perhaps the most gentlemanly proposal of the afternoon: **“If I were playing you this week Casey I would bench some of my players to make it fair, like a gentleman.”** This would have been a touching display of sportsmanship if it had not come from Chance Robinson, whose undefeated team is currently named **Njigba Please**, so the newsroom is not yet prepared to nominate him for the Walter Payton Man of the Year Award.

Casey falls to **0–4**, which is objectively not ideal. AAA Insider nevertheless notes that she has now endured four consecutive defeats without altering scores, changing lineups after kickoff or dissolving the league constitution. That is the kind of institutional stability voters rarely appreciate until it is gone.

Next week, Casey gets **Champ**, setting up a Hutchins-family matchup with Glen. Macon gets **la porta potty**, which means he goes from beating the league’s sitting commissioner to facing a 1–3 Louis Swanson.

One is a constitutional test.

The other is Louis.

## LAMBORGHINI MURCI 142.28, MANGLED PRISON POCKET 102.48
### *Larry promised punishment. He left without naming rights.*

Larry spent the buildup to Week 4 talking like a man preparing an execution. By Friday, he was publicly declaring that Ryan Hutchins needed to be punished for his “blasphemy,” and AAA Insider had already been informed that Larry’s private standard for success was not merely winning, but winning by **35 points**. ESPN even had Larry projected as the favorite entering the week. Four days later, Ryan Hutchins beat him **142.28–102.48**, cleared the league’s 35-point humiliation threshold and earned the right to rename Larry’s team. This is why AAA Insider generally recommends waiting until after the football games before establishing the terms of your own public degradation.

Hutchins had spent much of the week doing whatever the hell he was doing with his lineup, deliberately leaving players on the bench and insisting there was a strategy behind it. By Sunday afternoon, with the experiment somehow succeeding, he announced: **“The lineup smokescreen strategy seems to be working.”** It turns out the smokescreen was apparently designed to conceal **CeeDee Lamb scoring 41.3 points and Tee Higgins scoring 26.7**. Larry’s Ja’Marr Chase, meanwhile, produced **5.7**, meaning Hutchins got 21 more points from Cincinnati’s *other* receiver while Larry watched his first-round nuclear weapon turn into a decorative houseplant.

By mid-afternoon Hutchins was not merely beating Larry; he was physically at Larry’s house to observe the collapse. “I’m at his house, punishing him,” Hutchins reported, before issuing an important correction: **“Really JaMarr Chase is punishing him. I’m just here to watch from the corner chair.”** This is an objectively deranged way to spend a Sunday with a friend and therefore exactly the kind of league engagement AAA Insider supports.

Larry did receive **30.9 from Kenneth Walker III** and an absurd **19 points from kicker Will Reichard**, but the rest of the starting lineup responded as though they had been informed participation was optional. Trevor Lawrence scored 13.08. David Montgomery managed 4.3. Parker Washington contributed 2.0. Kansas City’s defense added 1.0. By Sunday night, Larry’s goal had undergone a significant strategic revision. The man who entered the week allegedly targeting a 35-point victory was now posting: **“We will fight to not lose by 35.”**

He lost by **39.80**.

That distinction matters, because in this league a loss of more than 35 points comes with consequences. Larry’s bench makes the result even more painful: **Malik Nabers scored 23.2, Tucker Kraft 16.5, Michael Wilson 16.5 and RJ Harvey 19.3**. The correct combination would have been enough to turn the beating into a win. Larry later offered the only airtight rebuttal available: **“Or if I had drafted a better team I wouldn’t need to worry about such trivial matters.”** Fair.

Hutchins, meanwhile, moved to **3–1**, an alarming development for a manager whose career résumé prominently features two Sackos and whose recent survival strategy has included openly discussing the sabotage of other teams to avoid collecting a third. AAA Insider is not yet prepared to call him good. We are, however, legally required to acknowledge that 3–1 is a much better record than whatever usually happens over there.

The real damage arrived Monday night. Hutchins formally instructed Larry to change his team name to **Mangled Prison Pocket** and supplied a new profile picture for the occasion. Shortly before that, while considering possible names, Hutchins observed: **“It’s so much easier to come up with names for Larry’s team than my own son.”** His son is due in November. AAA Insider sincerely hopes the naming process improves before then.

Next week, Hutchins gets Frankie’s **Coach** and a chance to reach 4–1. Larry gets **Bijan Mustard**, which means his first act as Mangled Prison Pocket will be facing Ryan Mays.

A difficult assignment.

AAA Insider will naturally be monitoring Larry very closely this week for entirely ordinary journalistic reasons.

## NJIGBA PLEASE 145.42, LA PORTA POTTY 126.98
### *The undefeated problem is becoming harder to explain away*

There are times in journalism when the evidence becomes too overwhelming to responsibly ignore. AAA Insider has reached that point with Chance Robinson. **He might actually be good at fantasy football this year.** We apologize to our readers.

Chance beat Louis **145.42–126.98**, improving to **4–0**, remaining the league’s only undefeated team and pushing his season total to a league-best **584.22 points**. At this stage, blaming the record on luck requires increasingly advanced mathematics. The newsroom has tried. Unfortunately, the numbers remain hostile.

Before going any further, however, we need to address the team name. **Njigba Please** is quite obviously an attempt to make an NFL player’s name sound uncomfortably close to a racial slur. AAA Insider’s official position is that the football team is 4–0 and the branding department is 0–4. We do not endorse it, we do not like it, and Chance will be judged every single time this newspaper is forced to type it. There are hundreds of Jaxon Smith-Njigba puns available. The man went directly for the one most likely to trigger an emergency meeting with Human Resources.

The irritating part is that Chance did not even need all of his players. **Tetairoa McMillan scored 45.2 points on his bench**, prompting Glen Hutchins on Tuesday to observe that leaving 45 points unused and still winning was “wild.” Correct. Chance essentially misplaced an entire elite fantasy performance and responded by scoring 145 anyway. Josh Allen gave him 18.52, Emanuel Wilson 27.0, Zay Flowers 25.8 and T.J. Hockenson 24.9. This was not a narrow escape. Chance left a small nation’s GDP on the bench and still posted the highest score of the week.

Louis, to his credit, actually had a very good Sunday. **Javonte Williams erupted for 31.3, Sam LaPorta added 22.4, Lamar Jackson scored 18.88 and Deebo Samuel gave him 18.0.** Mid-afternoon, even Chance admitted, **“Javonte Williams going to get Louis this win.”** For a brief and beautiful moment, the undefeated season appeared vulnerable.

It was not.

Louis finished with **126.98**, the fourth-highest score in the entire league this week, and still lost by 18.44. He would have beaten six of the other nine teams. Unfortunately, fantasy football does not award victories for being better than several unrelated people. Louis had the specific misfortune of being scheduled against the one asshole who scored 145.

Tuesday then provided one final indignity when Glen Hutchins, operating as the league’s self-appointed Captain Hindsight, announced that Louis could have beaten Chance by rearranging Deebo Samuel and Keaton Mitchell. Louis checked the arithmetic and responded: **“I still wouldn’t have won. Who taught you math.”** After further review, Louis was correct. Even the hypothetical lineup still loses. AAA Insider applauds Captain Hindsight for expanding his operation into **incorrect hindsight**, a previously underserved segment of fantasy analysis.

The larger concern is that Chance is starting to believe this. He has already circulated imagery depicting himself as a king, which is a fascinating choice for someone with longstanding commissioner ambitions. Most candidates at least pretend to support democratic institutions before commissioning the royal portrait. Chance appears to be campaigning on a platform of absolute power, selective enforcement and looking down upon the peasantry from a throne. AAA Insider appreciates the transparency.

Even Larry has begun wavering. On Tuesday he admitted, **“This might actually be Chance’s year,”** before clarifying, **“Not the Cowboys, but Chance specifically.”** That second sentence may be the meanest thing anyone said to a Cowboys fan all week.

Chance goes to **4–0** and gets Zak next, giving him an opportunity to reach 5–0 against a man who just lost to Ryan while scoring 89 points. Louis falls to **1–3** despite producing one of the week’s better scores and now gets Macon.

AAA Insider will continue searching for evidence that Chance is a fraud.

At present, the investigation is not going well.

## CHAMP 124.42, COACH 121.38
### *Frankie loses by 3.04 points, discovers Drake Maye approximately one day too late*

Week 4’s closest matchup ended with Glen escaping Frankie **124.42–121.38**, a margin of just **3.04 points** and exactly the kind of result designed to make a fantasy manager reopen the ESPN app several times in case math has changed.

Frankie actually entered the weekend in good shape. **Quinshon Judkins had already given him 21.6 on Thursday**, while Glen watched **Jaylen Warren score 15.6 from his bench**. By Sunday, though, Champ began assembling the deeply annoying kind of lineup where nobody completely explodes, but enough people keep scoring that the opponent slowly realizes death is approaching. **Jonathan Taylor scored 22.7, Brock Bowers 20.6, Brock Purdy 19.62 and Ashton Jeanty 18.6.** Even Cam Little contributed 14 from the kicker position because apparently Glen required assistance from every department.

Around late Sunday afternoon, Casey delivered the league’s official medical assessment:

> “RIP Frankie”

Ryan responded that Frankie does not read the chat, creating the unusual situation of the league holding a funeral while the deceased remained completely unaware he was dead. Later that night, Ryan Hutchins posted a video captioned, **“Frankie’s face when he checks his score at 5am tomorrow morning.”** This is one of the logistical problems with publicly humiliating Frankie: the man participates in GroupMe roughly on the same schedule as a comet. You can spend six hours talking shit about him and there is a legitimate possibility he will discover the conversation during breakfast three days later.

The actual tragedy was sitting directly on Frankie’s bench. **Drake Maye scored 26.16. Jared Goff scored 20.48.** The difference was **5.68 points**. Frankie lost by 3.04. On Monday night, Glen — currently operating under the increasingly confusing team name **Chris Turner** — informed Frankie that starting Maye would have won him the matchup.

For once, **Glen’s math was correct**.

AAA Insider would normally criticize a manager for leaving the winning quarterback on the bench, but Frankie resides in **Ohio**, a state best understood as a long-running administrative error between Pennsylvania and Indiana. It is entirely possible prolonged exposure has begun affecting decision-making. The loss is especially painful because Frankie’s lineup was otherwise respectable. **Chase Brown scored 19.1, Amon-Ra St. Brown 15.5, Minnesota’s defense 10**, and Judkins did his part before the weekend even started. Travis Kelce contributed a miserable **3.5**, but Frankie did not need a miracle. He needed one quarterback decision worth four points.

Glen, meanwhile, improves to **3–1** despite missing Casey and Ryan Hutchins’ baby shower after **spraining his ankle by slipping on something in his house**. AAA Insider has been unable to determine what caused the fall. Rug, toy, sock, structural defect and simple incompetence all remain under investigation.

The more important development comes next week, when Frankie faces **Ryan Hutchins** in what may be the league’s most emotionally complicated matchup. The two have been referred to as **“boyfriends”** for years, a friendship so committed that Ryan literally has **Frankie’s lips tattooed on his ass cheek**, and both men once got their **nipples pierced after losing a group bet**. AAA Insider is not here to define whatever this is. We are merely documenting it.

Now they have to play each other.

Frankie falls to **1–3**. Glen moves to **3–1**.

And somewhere in Ohio, there is presumably still time to start Drake Maye.

## THE WEEK 4 VERDICT

Week 4 leaves the league in a deeply unhealthy place. **Chance is 4–0**, Ryan Hutchins and Glen are somehow 3–1, and a four-team traffic jam at **2–2** now includes Ryan, Zak, Macon and the newly christened **Mangled Prison Pocket**. Frankie and Louis are 1–3, while Casey sits at 0–4 but continues to demonstrate the sort of restraint expected from public officials by refusing to simply edit the scores herself.

More importantly, **Ryan Mays has returned to .500**, which AAA Insider considers the central competitive development of the week. He accomplished this while defeating the subject of Friday’s profile, benching approximately 50 usable points, and spending part of the weekend beating Ryan Hutchins **101–106 on an actual golf course**. Hutchins later edged Ryan 52–53 in mini-golf, a result AAA Insider recognizes in the same way major sports outlets recognize preseason championships.

Week 5 now gives us Larry trying to recover from losing both a matchup and custody of his own team name against Ryan, Zak being fed directly into undefeated Chance, Glen facing the commissioner, and Ryan Hutchins meeting Frankie in an event that may require both a fantasy preview and relationship counseling. Meanwhile, Chance has already circulated another image of himself as a king, which is at least refreshingly transparent for a commissioner candidate. Most aspiring dictators wait until after the election to commission the royal portrait.

Four weeks are complete. Nobody has clinched anything, nobody has been eliminated, and there is still plenty of time for the competent teams to collapse and the bad teams to convince themselves they have figured something out.

Except Ryan.

**Ryan is clearly peaking at exactly the right time.**
