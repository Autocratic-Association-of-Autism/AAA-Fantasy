*By Ryan Mays — AAA Insider*

Five weeks into the 2026 fantasy football season, the Autocratic Association of Autism has an undefeated candidate for commissioner, a winless incumbent, and a disciplinary system that apparently consists of removing Glen Hutchins from GroupMe until he learns to behave.

At first glance, Chance Robinson's 5–0 record and Commissioner Casey Hutchins' 0–5 start might suggest the league needs new leadership. That would require believing fantasy football success is evidence of sound judgment, a theory Macon Moore's recent trade history has thoroughly disproven.

Casey delivered 166.52 points and was rewarded with a loss, Glen scored 174 and was rewarded with temporary exile, and Ryan Hutchins purchased airfare to Ohio for the privilege of getting beaten by Frankie Yentz in person. Elsewhere, Chance continued his undefeated march toward political power, Louis Swanson's roster continued resembling a hospital waiting room, and Larry Terrell managed to defeat a man who was clearly in no emotional condition to be playing fantasy football.

What began as another ordinary week of football ended with accusations of tyranny, questions about a man's recreational habits, and an emerging investigation into a possible children's amusement park empire.

**AAA Insider has reviewed the evidence. Things are considerably worse than we thought.**

## Champ 174.00, ChanceforChange2027 166.52
### A Historic Performance, a Constitutional Crisis, and a Possible Repetitive Stress Injury

Commissioner Casey Hutchins delivered one of the finest performances of the 2026 fantasy season, putting up an extraordinary 166.52 points while approaching the end of her pregnancy, managing a household, and running a league of grown men who apparently require more supervision than the child she's about to give birth to. Her husband, meanwhile, was in Ohio visiting Frankie Yentz, leaving Casey to manage everything back home. It was a remarkable display of strength, resilience, and leadership.

Naturally, she lost.

Glen Hutchins scored 174 points, largely thanks to an increasingly ridiculous collection of tight ends. Brock Bowers and Trey McBride combined for 44.6 points, further validating the concerns raised by Ryan Mays when Macon Moore inexplicably traded Bowers to Glen earlier this season. Mays warned that this was a terrible idea. Several league members disagreed. Those people were wrong. Mays was right. This is becoming a recurring theme, and frankly, it's exhausting being the only person in the league capable of recognizing an obvious disaster before it happens.

But Glen's football success has done little to distract from growing concerns about his personal life.

Glen's absence from his own brother's baby shower was already suspicious. Sunday's unsolicited confession about his activities during the Patriots game has now raised an uncomfortable question: Was missing the shower an isolated incident, or merely another casualty of Glen's increasingly demanding personal schedule? **AAA Insider cannot confirm that Glen is a compulsive masturbator, but his apparent commitment to the hobby is beginning to interfere with family obligations.**

Even his own brother, Ryan Hutchins, has begun asking questions about Glen's physical well-being: **“Sprained ankle or sprained wrist? That's the question.”**

Meanwhile, Casey found herself at the center of a constitutional crisis after exercising her authority to remove Glen from GroupMe. Twice. Critics have characterized the expulsions as authoritarian. AAA Insider sees a commissioner with the courage to make difficult decisions in the face of relentless stupidity. If pregnancy hormones played any role in her decision-making, they may be the most effective disciplinary mechanism this league has ever seen.

Casey has since declared herself **“the most democratic member of this league”** and warned that voting for Chance Robinson would be a vote for tyranny. These are strong words from a commissioner who has now demonstrated that freedom of speech does not necessarily include the freedom to annoy a pregnant woman indefinitely.

While Chance campaigns on promises of integrity, Casey is actually governing. Her administration has maintained order, enforced consequences, and demonstrated that no member of the Hutchins family is above the law.

Glen may have won the matchup, but Casey demonstrated something far more important than fantasy football success: leadership under extraordinary circumstances.

**The final score was 174–166.52. Casey lost a football game. Glen lost his GroupMe privileges. Only one of them came out of the weekend looking like an adult.**

<a id="week5-frankie-hutchins"></a>
## Coach 129.84, Lamborghini Murci 100.56
### Ryan Hutchins Flies to Ohio, Loses to Man Whose Lips Are Tattooed on His Ass

Ryan Hutchins spent the weekend in Ohio visiting Frankie Yentz, a man who has spent years demonstrating that participation in fantasy football is entirely optional. Armed with the right to forcibly bench one of Frankie's players thanks to a Week 1 wager, Hutchins removed Amon-Ra St. Brown from his opponent's lineup.

**Frankie won by 29.28 points.**

Hutchins managed to purchase airfare, abandon his heavily pregnant wife for the weekend, sabotage his opponent's lineup, and still get his ass kicked. Ryan already has Frankie's lips tattooed on his ass. Apparently that wasn't enough humiliation, so he paid to experience another one in person.

More interesting was Frankie's decision to leave Drake Maye's 39.46 points on the bench. Had he started Maye instead of Jared Goff, Frankie would have won by **39.80 points**, earning the right to rename Hutchins' team under the league's 35-point humiliation rule.

But Frankie is notoriously terrible at naming teams and frequently avoids the responsibility altogether. Did he deliberately bench Maye to avoid the stress of thinking up a funny name? His response to criticism, **“Didn't need him 😘,”** suddenly seems considerably more calculated. Frankie may have intentionally sacrificed fantasy points to avoid participating in fantasy football. It's perhaps the most Frankie Yentz thing that's ever happened.

Unfortunately, Frankie's apparent aversion to league participation is no longer his only controversy.

An anonymous tipster identifying himself as **Property Brotha Don'garious** has alleged that Frankie secretly operates Frankie's Fun Park in Raleigh, raising questions about suspicious birthday-party revenue and demanding his 2025 tax returns. The allegations are completely unsubstantiated.

AAA Insider has not investigated these claims, largely because an investigation would require Frankie to respond to a message, something the newsroom considers statistically impossible.

Instead, we may have no choice but to refer the matter directly to league leadership. Should a manager who refuses to participate, avoids his naming responsibilities, and now finds himself accused of financial crimes by an anonymous man named Property Brotha Don'garious continue occupying a valuable league spot?

With absolutely no evidence of wrongdoing, the league may have to reach a verdict based entirely on personal opinions, unfounded speculation, and whether anyone actually likes Frankie.

At least Frankie finally participated in GroupMe this weekend. Apparently 5G internet wasn't enough. It took Ryan Hutchins physically traveling to Ohio and supervising him.

**Frankie improves to 2–3. Hutchins falls to 3–2.** Ryan spent hundreds of dollars traveling across state lines to lose to a man who may have deliberately benched his best quarterback to avoid a minor administrative task.

**AAA Insider continues to await the tax returns.**

Unfortunately, Chance had Josh Allen, Jaxon Smith-Njigba, Derrick Henry, and, apparently, the greatest fucking kicker in America.

We're now at the point where Chance can make questionable lineup decisions, rely on a kicker to rescue him, and somehow emerge looking like a genius. It's infuriating.

<a id="week5-macon-louis"></a>
## Coke(r) Caine 129.44, La Porta Potty 100.52
### Macon Wins Despite His Best Efforts to Make a Terrible Trade

Macon Moore defeated Louis Swanson 129.44–100.52 this week, proving that even questionable roster management can occasionally produce positive results when Jahmyr Gibbs decides to score 37.7 points and Isaiah Likely unexpectedly contributes another 32.4.

Readers may recall that Macon recently traded Brock Bowers to Glen Hutchins for Dallas Goedert, a transaction that has since become the subject of considerable criticism from the only person intelligent enough to recognize the danger immediately: Ryan Mays. This week, Bowers scored 23 points for Glen while Goedert contributed 9.8 for Macon. Fortunately, Likely's 32.4-point performance spared Macon from having to confront the consequences of his own decisions.

It's a remarkable strategy. Trade away an elite tight end, watch him make another team considerably stronger, and then rely on an entirely different tight end to bail you out. **Macon basically handed Glen a flamethrower, then spent Sunday celebrating because his own house hadn't burned down yet. Congratulations, asshole. The rest of us live on the same fucking street.**

Louis, meanwhile, continues experiencing a season that increasingly resembles an insurance claim. With Lamar Jackson sidelined, Deebo Samuel leaving Sunday's game, and approximately half his roster requiring medical attention, Louis has been forced to place an unreasonable amount of faith in Spencer Shrader.

On Sunday, Louis announced that his kicker had been his highest-scoring player this season. Shrader promptly delivered 21 points, once again leading his team. Unfortunately, the Raiders defense contributed **negative six**, effectively taking 28.6% of Shrader's production and throwing it directly into the garbage.

Louis briefly expressed optimism in GroupMe, noting that half of Macon's team was on a bye and that he still had a chance. This optimism survived until Gibbs and Likely combined for 70.1 points and reminded everyone why hope is a dangerous emotion.

**Macon improves to 3–2, while Louis falls to 1–4.** Macon continues winning football games despite occasionally behaving like he's actively trying to lose them. Louis continues searching for healthy players, reliable starters, and perhaps a defense capable of scoring more points than an empty roster spot.

<a id="week5-ryan-larry"></a>
## Kenneth Skywalker 100.92, Big Ditka Energy 88.28
### Larry Terrell Defeats Grieving Man, Mistakes It for Accomplishment

Larry Terrell defeated Ryan Mays 100.92–88.28 this week, a result that will undoubtedly be celebrated in the Terrell household despite providing almost no evidence that Larry knows what he's doing.

Consider the lineup. Larry left Malik Nabers' 29.1 points and Parker Washington's 30.4 points on his bench while starting David Montgomery and RJ Harvey, who combined for an astonishing **1.8 points**. That's nearly 60 points sitting unused while two running backs contributed roughly the nutritional value of a single communion wafer.

And somehow, this idiot won.

Ryan, meanwhile, demonstrated exceptional managerial instincts under extraordinarily difficult circumstances. Facing a quarterback crisis, Mays scoured the waiver wire and secured Jacoby Brissett, the highest-scoring available option, who delivered an impressive 25.28 points. A last-minute decision to start Braelon Allen produced another 18.6. Two outstanding moves from a manager operating under tremendous emotional strain.

Unfortunately, Ryan was also dealing with the tragic passing of legendary Chicago Bears coach Mike Ditka. With an entire city in mourning, was it really reasonable to expect the Bears to travel to Green Bay and concentrate on football? Their 31–7 defeat suggests they were simply too devastated to perform. The Bears defense scored negative two fantasy points, while Colston Loveland contributed just 3.9. Some will call this poor roster construction. AAA Insider considers it a touching, if financially ruinous, tribute to a fallen legend.

Then there was Harrison Mevis, who contributed two miserable points before being cut by the Rams within 24 hours. It's not often a fantasy manager starts someone so terrible that an actual NFL franchise immediately agrees with his assessment.

Jameson Williams' 18.7 points on Ryan's bench deserve some consideration. Under normal circumstances, perhaps he gets the start. But expecting flawless decision-making from a man grieving Mike Ditka while watching his beloved Bears get dismantled by Green Bay seems borderline cruel. Frankly, Ryan deserves credit for remembering to submit a lineup at all.

**Larry improves to 3–2. Ryan falls to 2–3.** Larry left 59.5 points from two receivers on his bench, started two running backs who barely registered a pulse, and somehow walked away victorious against a man whose kicker was effectively fired for his performance.

Congratulations, Larry. You beat a grieving Bears fan by 12.64 points.

**There are victories worth celebrating, and then there's finding a wounded man on the side of the road and kicking him in the ribs. Larry appears very proud of himself.**

## The Final Word

Five games, five winners, and not a single victory by more than 35 points. For once, every manager escaped the weekend with their team name intact, although several emerged with considerably less dignity.

Casey remains winless but has demonstrated that she can run an entire league while dealing with circumstances that would send most of its members directly to injured reserve. Chance remains undefeated, despite a political platform consisting largely of repeating the word *integrity*. Glen remains Glen, although his GroupMe membership apparently depends on the commissioner's mood and the condition of his wrist.

Frankie's tax returns remain outstanding. Ryan Hutchins' Ohio expedition remains difficult to justify. And somewhere in North Carolina, Larry Terrell is celebrating a victory that probably warrants an apology.

**Week 5 is in the books. The standings are official. The constitutional crisis is ongoing.**
