*By Ryan Mays — AAA Insider*

AAA Insider believes fantasy football is about more than scores, standings and championships.

It is about people.

Behind every lineup is a manager. Behind every manager is a story. And behind some managers is an alarmingly small amount of evidence that they are actively participating in the league at all.

That is why AAA Insider is launching an occasional series introducing readers to the people who make this league what it is.

After an exhaustive, completely independent and unquestionably ethical selection process, our first subject is **Zak Wesolek**, manager of Weso-Hornay and, through a coincidence so extraordinary that lawyers have advised us not to dwell on it, the man currently playing Ryan Mays.

This is his story.

## Before Fantasy Football, There Was a Pole

Long before Weso-Hornay, before the Sacko, before Christian McCaffrey and before ESPN Fantasy became something Zak apparently checks whenever he gets around to it, there was pole vaulting.

AAA Insider's background investigation uncovered photographic evidence of a younger Zak competing for Norwich Free Academy in track and field.

There he stands: uniform on, pole in hand, staring down an obstacle that must be cleared through a precise combination of preparation, timing and athleticism.

Fantasy football would eventually ask considerably less of him.

The newsroom has been unable to determine whether pole vaulting provided any transferable skills to fantasy management, although both activities involve clearing a bar and occasionally failing in front of everyone you know.

Years later, Zak has traded the runway for the waiver wire.

He appears considerably less interested in running toward the latter.

## A Man on the Move

Zak is a travel nurse based in New England and recently spent several months working in Hawaii.

This provides valuable context for his fantasy-management philosophy.

While other managers spend football season refreshing injury reports, submitting waiver claims and sending increasingly desperate trade proposals at 11:47 p.m., Zak has apparently been traveling the country treating actual medical conditions and developing the radical belief that fantasy football is not important enough to ruin an afternoon.

Frankly, it is difficult to argue with him.

AAA Insider intends to anyway.

Zak is a male nurse, so he spends his workdays handling bodily fluids, getting called “doctor” by confused old people and explaining that, yes, men can be nurses too.

Somehow his dignity still reached its lowest point when **Glen for Sacko Champ actually became the Sacko**.

More on that shortly.

His time in Hawaii also explains his periodic disappearances from league discourse. At least one manager has successfully found a way to vanish for months at a time and classify it as employment.

The rest of the league has simply been calling it “not checking GroupMe.”

## The Quiet Professional

Zak joined the league in 2023 and has since developed one of its most distinctive communication strategies:

**Only speak when absolutely necessary.**

AAA Insider's GroupMe archive has recorded approximately **120 messages from Zak** since he entered the league conversation.

That is the fewest among the league's current managers.

By three messages.

His closest competition is Frankie.

So this is not an easy title to win.

Zak's most recent contribution came after defeating Casey in Week 2:

> “Appreciate the ez win.”

He has not spoken in GroupMe since.

AAA Insider has found no evidence that Zak lost his phone, joined a monastery or entered federal witness protection.

He simply appears to have said what needed saying.

Which, to his credit, was “ez win.”

## A Front Office Built on Doing Almost Nothing

If Zak's GroupMe presence is restrained, his approach to roster management borders on philosophical.

The official league archive lists **zero career trades** for Zak Wesolek.

Not zero this season.

Not zero successful trades after several failed negotiations.

**Zero.**

Three completed seasons. Fifty-plus matchups. Multiple playoff appearances. One championship game.

No trades.

Zak's first three seasons produced just **30 acquisitions** total:

- 8 in 2023
- 7 in 2024
- 15 in 2025

Through the first three weeks of 2026, he had made two more.

This season may be the purest expression yet of Zak's roster-building philosophy. He drafted 16 players.

As of Friday night, **15 of those 16 players remain on his roster**.

The lone casualty: Makai Lemon.

Jayden Daniels got hurt, so Zak added Tyler Shough. He added Evan McPherson.

Otherwise, the organization appears to believe that being selected by Weso-Hornay represents a binding employment contract.

Other managers call this “failing to churn the bottom of the roster.”

Zak calls it Tuesday.

There is also documented evidence that he is aware trades exist.

Last season, Chance sent Zak an offer and encouraged him to “peep” it.

Zak eventually responded:

> “Say no to trade rape!!”

The league database continues to show zero completed Zak Wesolek trades.

Talks appear to have broken down.

This remains one of the stranger contradictions AAA Insider uncovered during its investigation.

Zak is a male nurse, a profession requiring the confidence to walk into a room full of strangers, put on rubber gloves and ask deeply personal questions.

Yet apparently **sending a counteroffer on ESPN Fantasy is where he draws the fucking line.**

## 2024: The Year the Joke Became a Document

No serious examination of Zak's fantasy career can avoid 2024.

That season, Zak chose to name his team:

### **Glen for Sacko Champ**

The intent was obvious.

Glen was supposed to finish last.

This would prove to be an ambitious piece of speculative fiction.

By September, questions were already being raised about Zak's level of involvement.

Casey referred to Zak and Louis as “lax managers.”

Glen eventually offered a more technical evaluation:

> “Zak is literally taco and not playing lol he gonna be Sacko at this rate.”

AAA Insider reviewed the historical record.

Glen was correct.

Zak finished **10th**.

Glen finished ninth.

But fantasy football, like Greek tragedy, occasionally insists on resolving its themes with unnecessary precision.

Zak and Glen eventually met in the consolation ladder.

Glen beat him.

**223.22–203.80.**

The manager of a team named *Glen for Sacko Champ* had not merely become the Sacko himself.

He had done so while losing directly to Glen.

It is difficult to imagine a more complete humiliation without involving nudity or law enforcement.

League members responded with the compassion traditionally associated with this organization.

Zak was declared **“beer bitch.”**

Glen suggested the league rename its teams **“Zaks the Sacko.”**

At one point Chance briefly became confused about who had actually lost.

Glen clarified:

> “He’s a Wesolek.”

AAA Insider does not know what precisely this meant.

Nobody asked for clarification.

The newsroom nevertheless considers the 2024 case closed.

## Unfortunately, He Got Good

This investigation eventually encountered material damaging to its central thesis.

AAA Insider considered suppressing it.

Legal counsel advised otherwise.

Zak was actually very good in 2025.

After beginning his fantasy career with consecutive **4–10 seasons**, he went **9–5**, scored **1,876.22 regular-season points**, earned the No. 3 playoff seed and reached the league championship.

He lost that championship to Glen.

By **1.88 points**.

This technically makes Zak a former league runner-up.

We checked twice.

The transformation was dramatic.

2023: 4–10.  
2024: 4–10 and Sacko.  
2025: championship appearance.

Apparently all Zak needed to become competitive was for everyone to spend an entire year calling him incompetent.

There is also one particularly unpleasant fact involving the author of this article.

In Week 14 last season, Zak scored **183.88 points against Ryan Mays**.

That remains Zak's career high and one of the highest weekly scores preserved in the league archive.

Ryan scored 105.30.

Zak won by **78.58**.

AAA Insider has examined the possibility that this game was incorrectly entered into the historical database.

It was not.

The newsroom deeply regrets the accuracy of its own archival standards.

Fortunately, there is opposing precedent.

In 2023, Ryan defeated Zak **170.50–86.04**.

Margin: **84.46 points**.

That remains one of the largest ass-kickings in league history.

So far, Ryan–Zak matchups have operated less like football games and more like custody exchanges where one parent leaves with absolutely everything.

## The 2026 Contender, If We Must Call Him That

Zak enters Week 4 at **2–1**.

That record places Weso-Hornay fifth in the league.

On its face, respectable.

AAA Insider requested additional context.

Zak has scored **351.90 points**.

His opponents have scored **354.12**.

He therefore possesses the increasingly prestigious combination of a **winning record and negative point differential**.

His two victories came against Casey and Louis.

Those teams entered the weekend with a combined record of **1–5**.

His loss came against Macon, who dropped **162.50 points** on him in Week 1.

AAA Insider is not alleging fraud.

We are merely noting that Weso-Hornay's 2–1 record has been assembled under market conditions that would make a subprime mortgage broker nostalgic.

## The Current Crisis

Which brings us to the reason AAA Insider happened to become interested in Zak this particular week.

Again, entirely coincidental.

Thursday night is complete.

Zak started Michael Pittman Jr.

Pittman scored **3.5 points**.

Ryan started Cleveland's defense.

Cleveland scored **8**.

As of the latest ESPN snapshot:

### Bijan Mustard 8.0

### Weso-Hornay 3.5

ESPN's remaining projection currently has Ryan ahead approximately **121.68–114.38**.

It is extremely early.

No responsible journalist would draw conclusions from a 4.5-point Thursday lead.

AAA Insider understands this.

AAA Insider has nevertheless published approximately 1,500 words about Zak Wesolek.

## So, Who Is Zak Wesolek?

He is a former pole vaulter.

A travel nurse.

A man who spent months working in Hawaii while the rest of us presumably sat at home refreshing questionable injury tweets.

He is the least frequent GroupMe contributor among the league's current managers.

He has never completed a fantasy trade.

He still owns 15 of the 16 players he drafted this season.

He went 4–10.

Then 4–10 again.

He named his team after Glen becoming Sacko.

He then became the Sacko himself.

By losing to Glen.

He responded by nearly winning the championship the following year.

And now he is 2–1 despite being outscored.

There may actually be something admirable buried underneath all of this.

Zak's career suggests that fantasy football does not necessarily require endless waiver claims, trade negotiations, spreadsheets, podcasts, projections or thousands of GroupMe messages.

Perhaps you really can draft a team, occasionally replace someone who becomes physically incapable of playing football, disappear for several days and trust Christian McCaffrey to handle the rest.

It has worked substantially better than AAA Insider would like to admit.

But years after Zak made a sport out of clearing bars, another one now sits in front of him.

**Ryan Mays.**

And this time, Zak does not have a pole.

He has Michael Pittman's 3.5 points.

Ryan is paying attention.

And through another completely unrelated coincidence, **so is the press.**
